1. The commercial problem in one breath

Organizations often approve an AI model by name. A name is not a revision. Supplier evidence, private evaluation, restrictions, and the decision usually live in different places — so later you cannot reconstruct what was approved, or detect that a different revision arrived under the same marketing name.

QEV’s commercial wedge is not “another model registry.” It is a portable, signed decision object bound to an exact artifact or model revision.

2. What the process produces

External model or project
→ exact identity / revision pin
→ supplier (public) evidence
→ private evaluation (encrypted)
→ restrictions / policy outcome
→ signed dossier (.qevpack)
→ later verify · tamper · revision mismatch

QEV can establish

  • Integrity of signed dossier contents
  • That a mathematical signature verifies
  • Separate trust states (integrity, issuer, revocation, policy)
  • That private evidence is present and locked
  • That a different revision is not the approved one

QEV does not claim

  • That a model is “safe”
  • That a model card is true
  • Enterprise production readiness
  • Formal third-party audit
  • Legal notarization

5. Step-by-step — commercial wedge (Model Intake)

1

Pivot language

“Same process, institutional workflow: external model intake.”
2

Tiny GPT-2 → REJECTED

Pin exact revision. Supplier evidence incomplete. Decision is rejection — not a safety score, a policy outcome on recorded evidence.

3

SmolLM2 → SANDBOX ONLY

Better documentation/lineage, still not full production approval. Shows graduated outcomes, not binary hype.

4

Private evaluation stays private

Encrypted internal evaluation can bind to that revision without publishing red-team notes.

5

Approval does not transfer across revisions

If the revision changes, the old dossier remains valid for its pin; the new revision is unreviewed until a new decision exists.

This is the money sentence for commercial and SBIR audiences.

6. Trust states to name out loud

  • Integrity — fingerprints match; content was not altered after signing
  • Authenticity / signature — the signature verifies under the stated key model
  • Issuer trust / revocation — a package can be intact yet untrusted if the issuer is revoked
  • Policy outcome — REJECTED / SANDBOX ONLY / approved classes from evidence rules, not model IQ

Collapsing these into one “trusted” bit is how real audits go wrong. QEV keeps them separate.

8. What “commercially needed” means here

  • Shadow AI and untracked Hugging Face pins
  • Audit reconstruction time after an incident or customer request
  • Silent model drift under the same product name
  • Need to keep eval notes confidential while still binding a decision

Registries, GRC tools, and model hosts remain complementary. QEV’s hypothesis is the portable decision package that can leave the platform.
