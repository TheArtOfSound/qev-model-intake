The real-world problem

Someone says “we already approved that model.” Nobody can prove what that meant.

Outside models show up fast: open downloads, vendor APIs, agent tools. Approval usually lives in email, chat, or a wiki page. That works until an incident, an audit, a customer question, or a quiet model update.

Name ≠ version

“We use Llama / the Hugging Face model” is not a record. The bits can change under the same product name.

Evidence is scattered

Public docs on a website. Test notes in Drive. The yes/no in Slack. Later, nobody can rebuild the package.

Quiet drift

A new version lands. Yesterday’s approval is treated as still valid. No clear line that says “this is different — review it again.”

How it works

A simple workflow your team can actually run.

No need to replace Hugging Face, your registry, or your ticketing tool. QEV sits where the decision is made and produces a portable record.

1

Start with the thing you care about

An outside AI model, or any important project package you need to approve.

2

Lock the exact version

Not “the model.” The specific version your people actually reviewed.

3

Attach what you used to decide

Public docs and supplier materials. Private test notes can stay sealed inside.

4

Record the decision and keep the package

Rejected, limited use, or approved for a path you define. Later you can check that the package still matches and see what was decided.

Straight talk

What QEV is not.

We will not sell you a fairy tale

  • QEV does not score whether a model is “safe.”
  • QEV does not guarantee a supplier’s public docs are true — it records what you used.
  • Your organization still owns testing and the final call.
  • We do not claim finished enterprise suite status on day one.

That honesty is deliberate. Teams adopt tools they can explain to their boss without wincing. QEV’s job is the approval record — not magic judgment.
